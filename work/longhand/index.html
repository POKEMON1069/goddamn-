Case 02 — Editorial platform

Longhand

A twelve-year archive of long-form essays, rebuilt so that the footnotes — the part readers came for — became the navigation.

Demonstration case study — fictional client

Client
Fictional brief, written for this build
Sector
Independent publishing
Scope
Reading interface, archive IA, print styles
Team
One designer, one engineer, one editor
Modelled window
9 weeks
01

Context

The archive held roughly 900 essays, many with dozens of footnotes and source notes. On the old site, footnotes were links that jumped to a list at the end — a pattern that works in print because your thumb holds the page. On a phone it meant abandoning the sentence.

Analytics told a familiar story: strong entry traffic, short sessions, and almost no movement between essays. The archive looked shallow to readers who had actually come for depth.

Constraints

Legacy markup. Poor mobile networks. Reading must survive without scripts.

02

Constraint

The real limits

  • Content debt. Twelve years of hand-written HTML with inconsistent heading levels.
  • Bandwidth. A meaningful share of readers arrived on connections where a 400 KB page took seconds.
  • Editorial trust. The editor would not accept a reading experience that could break when scripts failed.
  • Print. Academic readers still printed essays; the print stylesheet had to carry citations.
03

Decisions

Margin notes that become asides.

On wide screens a note sits in the margin, aligned to its sentence; on narrow screens the same markup renders as a styled aside directly after the paragraph. Trade-off: no sticky side-panel navigation inside essays, which some readers expected.

A locked measure, a fluid scale.

The text column stays between 62 and 68 characters regardless of viewport, while headings scale with clamp(). Trade-off: fewer dramatic full-bleed moments; a calmer page that keeps a consistent line length at every size.

Static HTML over a reading app.

Pre-rendered pages, one small script for progress and search, no client-side routing. Trade-off: no page transitions; in exchange, first paint arrives before the script does and the archive can be mirrored by libraries.

04

Craft

Typography

A serif for reading, a narrow sans for navigation, and a monospace used only for citation numbers and dates. Three roles, no exceptions — the archive has enough variation in its content without the interface adding more.

Details that carry the experience

  • Footnote markers use tabular figures so they do not shift the baseline.
  • An auto-generated contents list on long essays, hidden from screen readers when empty.
  • Link underlines that thicken on hover instead of changing colour, preserving a single accent.
  • Print CSS that expands citation URLs and removes the ornament layer.
  • Reading progress expressed as a one-pixel rule, not a floating pill.

Nothing in the reading experience requires JavaScript: the notes, the contents list and the archive pagination are all in the served HTML.

Performance budget

Under 200 KB per essay on first load, fonts included.

05 — Signals

What we would measure.

No results are claimed for this written brief. These are the observations that would tell us whether the reading experience had actually changed.

  • S1

    Notes opened per essay

    The behaviour the design exists to encourage, counted before and after.

  • S2

    Essays read after the first

    Depth of archive use, rather than raw session length.

  • S3

    Time to first paragraph

    Measured on a throttled connection, on the oldest and heaviest essays.

  • S4

    Print events and citation copies

    Small numbers, but they tell us the scholarly readers stayed.
